What Shor and Grover teach a vibration sensor

Quantum algorithms will not run on a bearing. Their internal structure, period finding, phase-coherent interference, rational snapping, amplitude amplification, transfers completely. Every chart below is real output of the pipeline, and one color rule holds everywhere:

phase-locked to the shaft (imbalance, misalignment, gears) real but drifting (bearing tones, 1 to 2% slip) noise

1The shaft is a clock

Shor's algorithm finds the period of a a^x mod N sequence. A rotating machine hands you its period for free: one tachometer pulse per revolution. Every fault frequency is best written not in Hz but in orders, multiples of shaft speed. Below, 0.25 s of a machine running near 30 Hz whose speed wanders by 1.5%: the vibration signal with tacho pulses marked, and the speed profile over 8 s.

One synthetic run: 1x and 2x locked tones plus a 2.998x bearing tone with slip in noise. Teal ticks are tacho pulses, the machine's own clock.

3Interference: the Shor trick

Shor's speedup comes from interference: amplitudes at the true period add constructively, everything else self-cancels. Classically: cut the angular signal into blocks of exactly 5 revolutions and average the complex spectra. Locked components keep the same phase in every block and survive. Noise and drifting tones arrive with random phase and cancel as 1/√N. Drag N:

Solid: coherent (complex) average. Faint: power average, which keeps everything. Watch the floor and the amber 2.998x peak sink while teal peaks at 1x and 2x stand still.

4The coherence ratio, one number per line

Divide the two spectra: coherent over power. Near 1 means locked, near 0 means drifting or noise. This one ratio separates a 3x harmonic from a 2.998x bearing tone even when frequency resolution cannot tell them apart. Pick a machine condition:

Bars: coherence ratio per order bin (only bins with real energy shown solid). The fingerprint is readable by eye: imbalance is one teal spike at 1x, misalignment a teal 1-2-3 family, bearing runs show energy whose ratio collapses.

5Continued fractions: Shor's post-processing

Shor's final classical step snaps a noisy phase measurement to an exact rational p/q. Fault orders are rationals of small denominator: 1/2 from looseness, 2/5 from a cage, 2/1 from misalignment. Bearing orders like 2.998 are near-rational and refuse to snap, which is itself the signature. Slide a measured order and watch the convergents:

Tolerance 0.01, denominator limit q ≤ 8, the same rule the pipeline uses. Try 0.403, 0.503, 2.001, then park on 2.998.

6Grover: rotate until the target aligns

Grover's algorithm amplifies a marked state by repeated rotation. Two classical descendants close the gap section 3 left open, because coherent averaging destroys bearing tones.

6a, slip scan. Hypothesize a slip s, demodulate at 2.998·(1+s), sweep s. The curve peaks exactly at the true slip: the frame has been rotated until the marked component aligns. Peak height detects the bearing, peak position measures slip, a free load indicator.

6b, predictive alignment (PPA). A bearing tone's phase random-walks, so blocks cancel. But its phase is predictable one block ahead. Align each block by the previous block's phase: a real tone survives, noise does not. The phasor clock shows 24 block phasors; press align.

8The referee: how the loop measures success

An autonomous research loop will fool itself if allowed: iterate long enough and any benchmark improves by luck. The promotion gate demands that a candidate beat the incumbent with confidence alpha = 0.05 / attempts, so the evidence bar rises every time the loop tries something. Everything else in the loop serves the same principle: a hash-sealed 20% vault of runs the iterations can never touch (3 lifetime evaluations), an append-only ledger where kills count as much as promotions, and guardrails that block any promotion that regresses the tacho baseline, skips its mechanism test, or cannot run on the edge in under 5 s.
